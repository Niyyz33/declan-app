Check it like a pro 🔍

Go through the phone in this order, every time.

If it doesn’t turn on, don’t buy it.
If you’re in doubt, deduct. It’s always better to deduct than to guess.
Not in the phone yet? See how to get in fast →
1

Battery

Go to Settings › Battery › Battery Health & Charging.

Under 85% is a deduction. Under 80% you always deduct it. The amount is in the lookup further down.

2

Parts and Service History

Go to Settings › General › About › Parts and Service History.

If it says Unknown Part, or it shows a replaced battery or screen, that is a third-party part, so deduct it.

3

The screen

Tilt the screen under a light and look closely. Is it cracked, scratched or clean?

CrackedBroken screen
ScratchedAlso a broken screen deduction
CleanNo deduction

Cracked means cracks. Scratched means fine lines you can see in the light.

The fingernail test

Slide your fingernail across the mark, slowly. If your nail catches, it’s a deep scratch. If in doubt, deduct.

A scratched or third-party screen counts as a broken screen. To sell it, the screen needs replacing.
4

The cameras

Open the Camera app and check the front and back cameras both work.

Smashed lens: $20 each. Camera exposed (lens gone): $50 each. A camera that doesn’t work is its own deduction.

5

Frame and back

Turn the phone over. A cracked back is a deduction.

Now check the frame. Look along the edge and run your finger round it. Chips and scratches deduct. Bent deducts too.

Straight means a flat edge. Bent means you can see a bow or a gap.

6

Face ID, speaker and buttons

Face ID
Speaker
Buttons

Test each one. If one doesn’t work, it’s a deduction.
