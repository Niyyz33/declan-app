Selling on Marketplace 🛒

Photos, title, groups, and what to say.

The app writes your title and description. You just copy and paste them.
1

Get the phone ready

1
Make sure it’s charged past 20%.
2
Go through the whole phone once more. Anything wrong goes in the listing, so check first.
2

Take the photos

Take about 7 photos. Start with the back, held in your hand. That’s the cover photo. The rest can go in any order.

Quick rules

☀️
Daylight. Near a window. Don’t use the flash, it causes glare.
🟫
Plain background. A table or bench, nothing messy.
🔍
Show every mark. If it has a scratch, take a close-up. It stops arguments at the meetup.
📱
Take them with your phone upright, so the photos aren’t sideways.
3

Copy the title and description

The app writes these for you. Don’t write your own.

1
Open the Phones tab and find the phone.
2
Tap 📋 Facebook description.
3
Pick the Condition. Tick No cracks or major damage only if it’s true. Add what it comes with, and your pickup suburb.
4
Tap Copy on the Title, then paste it into Facebook. Do the same for the Description.
Don’t change the words. Only say what the app says. If something’s wrong, fix it in the app and copy again.
4

Set the price

Price it about $20 above the lowest you’d take. That leaves room to drop $20 when someone asks.

Have a look at what the same phone is listed for on Marketplace near you. Don’t be the cheapest, and don’t be $50 above everyone.

If you’re not sure, ask Ethan before you post.

5

Post it on Marketplace

1
Open Facebook, tap Marketplace, then Sell, then Item for sale.
2
Add the photos. Put the back photo first, it’s the cover.
3
Paste the title. Type the price. Pick the category Mobile Phones.
4
Set the condition (usually Used – Like New), then paste the description.
5
Check the location is right, then tap Publish.

The menu names move around a little. Pick the closest one.

Not selling after about a week? Open the listing, tap the three dots, and tap Renew listing so it shows as new again. Ask Ethan before dropping the price by more than $20.
6

Find and join buy, swap and sell groups

Local buy, swap and sell groups put your phone in front of lots more people. Here’s how to find the good ones.

1
Open Facebook and tap the search bar. Type buy swap sell and your city, like buy swap sell Melbourne. Tap Groups to see only groups.
2
Try again with the suburbs near where you meet people, like buy sell Footscray. Names are different everywhere, so try a few searches.
3
Open a group and check it’s a good one: lots of members, and new posts every day. A quiet group is a waste of time.
4
Read the About section and the rules. Skip the group if it says no business posts or no resellers.
5
Tap Join. Many groups ask a few questions. Answer honestly. Then wait. It can take a few hours or a few days. Don’t ask over and over.

Which ones to join

Join as many as you can, and start with the ones with the most members. The member count shows under the group’s name in the search results.

Big city-wide groups first, then the suburbs near your meetup spots. More groups means more people see your phone.

Join a few each day instead of loads at once, so Facebook doesn’t think you’re spamming.

If a group looks good but has rules you’re not sure about, ask Ethan before posting.

Posting in a group

1
Open your Marketplace listing, tap Share, and choose the group.
2
Some groups only allow posts on certain days, or after you’ve been a member for a while. Follow what they say.
3
Post it once per group. Don’t post the same phone over and over.
7

Replying to messages

Reply fast. Same-day buyers are the best ones.

When they say “Is this still available?”

Hey, yep still available! Are you able to come today?

When someone offers way too low, say this, one time.

Sorry, I’ve got a lot of people interested at the moment for the full price, however if you wanted to come today I can do $XXX.Put in the price. Go about $20 under your asking price.

If they keep pushing, say:

Sorry mate, that’s the best I can do. Let me know if you change your mind 👍
Don’t argue. Don’t go any lower. Another buyer will come along.

Keep chatting inside Facebook Messenger. If they want to move to text or WhatsApp straight away, be careful.

8

Watch out for scams

Never send money to anyone, for any reason.
⚠️
“I paid too much, send me the difference.” It’s a scam. The payment never happened.
📸
A screenshot or email that says they paid. Ignore it. Only your own banking app counts.
🔢
“Send me the code you just got.” Never give anyone a code sent to your phone.
🚚
“My courier or mate will collect it.” No. You hand it to the person you’re talking to.
📧
An email “from PayID” asking you to pay to release the money. PayID never does that.
9

The meetup

1
Meet somewhere busy and well lit, or somewhere you’re comfortable. Don’t give your address until they’ve said they’re coming.
2
Let them check the phone. You showed every mark in your photos, so nothing will be a surprise.
3
Keep the phone in your hand while they look at it. Don’t let anyone walk off with it to “test it”. Cash is best. Count it before you let go.
4
If they want to pay by bank transfer, wait until the money shows in your own banking app. Not a screenshot, not an email.
5
Make sure there’s no Apple ID signed in. If you added anything, go to Settings › General › Transfer or Reset iPhone › Erase All Content and Settings first.
6
If it feels wrong, walk away. You can message Ethan after.
10

After it sells

1
Delete the listing completely on Facebook. Don’t just mark it as sold. If you shared it into groups, delete those posts too.
2
In the app, open Phones, find the phone, tap 💰 Mark sold, type what it sold for and tap Save.
