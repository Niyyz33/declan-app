The business

Cash for Phones buys used iPhones for cash from people in your city. Ads bring in sellers, we agree a price range with them by message, and you meet them in person to check the phone and pay. You don’t find sellers or negotiate over chat.

What you do

  1. Meet sellers

    Meetups are booked into your calendar from your free times, always at the same public spot.

  2. Inspect the phone

    Follow the same checklist every time: parts and service history, battery, screen, back, frame, cameras, Face ID and charging.

  3. Say the price once

    The app gives you the quote and the deduction for each fault. You say the final number, then pay cash.

  4. Stay safe with locked phones

    You never sign in with a seller’s Apple ID. If a phone is locked, won’t turn on, or the seller won’t erase it, you walk away.

  5. Log it

    Log every phone in the app straight after the meetup.

  6. Handle the two kinds of phone

    Damaged (any fault at all): keep it safe, then post it to us in a box with a label we send. Mint (nothing wrong): you sell it yourself on Facebook Marketplace and share the listing into local groups.

  7. Finish properly

    Ask if they have other phones to sell, hand over a business card and ask for a Google review.

You’re not responsible for ads, chat messages, prices, bookings or accounts. A virtual assistant and the owner handle those.

A way to reach the meetup spot

A car is not needed.

Confidence with cash and strangers

A Facebook account

One with some history, for selling mint phones.

A few steady hours

Most meetups take 20 to 30 minutes plus travel.

$2,000 covers roughly eight or nine phones at the average price before your first repayment, so you post a box as soon as you have five damaged phones.

You don’t need any experience buying phones. Training covers the whole process, including practice meetups.

How you get paid

  • Damaged phones: $30 for every phone, going up to $35 for each one after your 35th in a month. You’re also paid back exactly what you paid the seller.
  • Mint phones: you keep the sale money after paying the seller, minus a set commission to us that depends on the profit on that phone. For example $45 on a $100 to $149 spread, or $75 on a $200 to $249 spread.
  • Repayment on damaged phones: half when you post the box, half when it arrives.
  • Statements: your fees and commission go on a fortnightly statement.

Safety

Always meet in a busy public place in daylight. Count the cash and check the phone before you hand it over. Walk away from any phone or person that doesn’t feel right. You never have to complete a meetup.
