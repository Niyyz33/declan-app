1. The job

We buy used iPhones from people who want to sell. There are two kinds:

  • Damaged phones (most of them): you buy it and it comes to Ethan. Ethan pays you back what you paid, plus $30 per phone.
  • Mint phones (nothing wrong, or just a worn battery): you buy it and sell it yourself on Facebook Marketplace. You keep the sale money, and a set commission goes on your fortnightly statement. The app works it out.

Buyers come to you. Ethan runs the ads, talks to the sellers, gives the quote and books every meetup, then sends you the seller’s name and number. Everyone meets you at Coles North Ward. Never anywhere else.

2. The life of one phone

  • Ethan books it and your phone buzzes.
  • An hour before, you text the seller to check they’re still coming.
  • When you arrive, you text them again.
  • You inspect the phone, say the price and pay cash.
  • Afterwards, on your own, you log it in the app. Never in front of the seller.
  • Damaged phones: keep them safe at home until Ethan collects. Mint phones: list and sell.

3. Your day

Every morning at 8am the app asks what time you’re free. Answer it, even if it’s a day off. Your first one starts Saturday 21 November. Open the app from the icon on your Home Screen, never from Safari.

4. The app

  • Home: your next meetup.
  • Meetups: Confirm sends the one-hour text, I’ve arrived sends the arrival text, Log this phone, and No sale or No show if it falls through.
  • Log: tap in the phone’s details and the price you paid.
  • Phones: everything you’ve bought. Count the phones in your hand against this list every evening.

5. Inspecting a phone (same order every time)

  • Get into the phone: never sign in to an Apple ID, skip it. Turn on your hotspot (a wiped phone has no Wi-Fi). If the Home Screen shows, ask the seller to erase it in front of you.
  • Settings, General, About: check the model, storage, SIM lock, and Parts and Service History (look for “Unknown Part”).
  • Battery Health: under 85% deducts, under 80% always.
  • Check the screen, both cameras, the frame and back, Face ID, speaker, buttons and the charging port.
  • If in doubt, deduct.

6. Do not buy it, ever

  • It won’t turn on.
  • It’s Activation Locked.
  • It hasn’t been erased.
  • Something feels wrong.

Say: “Sorry, it’s not for me,” and walk away. Only go to a meetup once the seller has replied to your one-hour text.

7. Staying safe, and selling mint phones

  • Meet somewhere busy and well lit. Check the phone before you pay and count the cash out in front of them. If it feels wrong, walk away.
  • Mint phones: take about 7 photos in daylight, copy the listing from the app, never go under the lowest price. When it sells, delete the listing and tap Mark sold.

8. Google reviews

After every meetup, once they’ve been paid, say:

“Just before you go, we’ve recently been trying to grow our Google review page. Is it okay if I quickly walk you through a Google review?”

Then use the review link in the app.
