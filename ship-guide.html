Meet Bubbles. Follow along and your phones will arrive in one piece, every time.

How many phones are you sending?
5
1

Print the label

Ethan will send you the postage label. Print it on plain A4 paper.

Check the barcode is dark and sharp, and nothing is cut off at the edges.

2

Grab your gear

Buy your bubble wrap sleeves at the Post Office. They're $1.30 each. Each sleeve protects up to 3 phones. The counter above tells you how many to get. You don't need your own tape or pen. The Post Office does the taping and gives you a pen to sign.
3

Wrap the phones

The golden rule: 3 phones is the most you can put in one sleeve. If you have more, finish one bundle, then start again with a fresh sleeve.
Got more than 3 phones? Repeat steps A to E with a new sleeve. Put the highest model in each new sleeve first, just like before.
4

Pack the box

Small box: up to 9 phones (that's 3 bundles). Lay the bundles flat, side by side, so the lid closes with no bulge.

Medium box: for 10 phones or more. Lay the bundles flat in neat layers, side by side.

Shake test: close the lid and give the box a gentle shake. If anything moves or rattles, add a little scrunched paper or spare bubble wrap until it's snug.

At the counter

🏷️
Bring your printed label. The Post Office person tapes it onto the box for you. Keep it flat and clean so the barcode scans.
✍️
Sign the declaration. They'll give you a pen. Sign the small "Sign here" box on the lid. The box can't be sent without it.
🧷
No tape needed. The Post Office tapes the box shut for you.
5

Post it

Take the box and your printed label to any Post Office and hand them over at the counter. They'll tape up the box, stick on the label and give you a pen to sign. Ask them to scan it, and keep the receipt.

Then open the app and tap “I’ve posted it” on the Ship screen, so Ethan knows it's on its way.
