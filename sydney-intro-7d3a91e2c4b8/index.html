1. What it is

Cash for Phones buys used iPhones from regular people who want to sell fast for cash. We then sell the phones on to other buyers. Our edge: a quick reply, a simple meetup, cash on the spot, and we take phones that are damaged, which most people struggle to sell.

Think of it as a local buyback service. It already runs in Townsville and Brisbane, and Melbourne is starting. Sydney would be the next city.

2. How a phone moves through it

Ad. Ethan runs online ads in your city. People who want to sell message us.
Quote. Ethan chats with the seller, finds out the model and any damage, and gives a price.
Booking. If they agree, Ethan books a meetup and your phone gets a notification with the seller’s name, number and the phone.
Meetup. You meet at one set, busy public spot. It takes about five minutes.
Inspect and pay. You check the phone against a checklist, then pay cash.
Log. Afterwards you record it in the app, on your own.
Onward. The phone goes on to a buyer. Details below.

3. Who does what

Ethan (owner)
  • Runs all the ads
  • Talks to every seller and gives the quote
  • Books every meetup
  • Sets the prices and the rules
  • Builds and fixes the app
  • Finds buyers for the phones
You (Sydney partner)
  • Say when you’re free
  • Go to the meetups
  • Inspect the phone and decide yes or no
  • Pay the seller
  • Log everything in the app
  • Look after the phones you buy

You never write ads or negotiate prices. Ethan does that part. Your job is the five minutes in person.

4. The two kinds of phone

  • Damaged phones (most of them): cracked screen, bad battery, dead camera and so on. You buy it and it goes to Ethan, who pays you back what you paid plus a set fee per phone. You do not sell these yourself.
  • Mint phones (nothing wrong, or just a worn battery): you buy it, then sell it yourself on Facebook Marketplace. You keep the sale money, and a set commission goes to Ethan. The app works that out.

5. What a meetup looks like

  • An hour before, you text the seller to check they’re still coming.
  • You arrive, text them, and meet.
  • You check the phone in the same order every time: it turns on, it’s been erased, it isn’t locked to someone’s Apple ID, the model and storage are right, the battery health, the screen, the cameras, the buttons.
  • Good phone: you say the price and pay cash. Not right: you politely say “Sorry, it’s not for me” and walk away.
  • Then you ask for a Google review, and log the phone in the app after they’ve left.

We teach this step by step in Training 1 and 2. You don’t need to know any of it now.

6. The app

There is a simple app made for partners. It shows your next meetup, sends the check-in texts for you, and holds the phone log, the inspection guides and your statements. Every morning at 8am it asks what time you’re free that day, and Ethan only books meetups into the times you give.

It runs on a phone and is added to the Home Screen like a normal app. It was built and tested on iPhone. We will check it works on whatever phone you have.

7. Your time

  • You choose when you’re free. Lectures and exams come first.
  • A meetup is about five minutes plus travel. Some days there are none, some days several.
  • Volume depends on the ads and the week. It is not a fixed number every day.
  • You need to reply to texts promptly and turn up when you say you will.

8. Money, in plain terms

  • You use your own cash to buy the phones. You need money set aside to float, and you get paid back as phones move on. How much depends on the city and how busy it is.
  • Damaged phones: paid back in full plus a set amount per phone.
  • Mint phones: you keep what you sell for, minus a set commission.
  • You get a statement every fortnight showing what was bought and what you’re owed.
  • The exact terms are agreed with Ethan and written down before you start.

No promises on income. It depends on how many phones come through.

9. What you need

  • You are trustworthy and reliable. You handle other people’s cash.
  • Starting cash for buying phones, agreed with Ethan.
  • A way to get around Sydney to the meeting spot.
  • A smartphone with data. An iPhone makes the job easier (see next box).
  • A Facebook account, for selling the mint phones.
  • A safe place at home to keep damaged phones until they’re collected or posted.

10. No iPhone? That’s a problem to solve, not a dealbreaker

Everything we buy is an iPhone, and the inspection is done on the phone itself (settings, battery health, Face ID, erase state). You need to be comfortable with iPhones.

  • For learning, you can use a family member’s or friend’s iPhone.
  • For the job, the simplest fix is a cheap used iPhone of your own. It also runs the app.
  • If you stay on Android, Ethan checks the app works on it first.

We decide this together before Training 1.

11. Rules that never change

We never buy a phone that won’t turn on, that’s Activation Locked, that hasn’t been erased, or when something feels wrong. We meet in busy, well lit, public places. We check the phone before paying. You walk away if it feels off. These rules protect your money.

12. The honest risks

  • You put your own money in. A mistake on a phone can cost you, which is why the checklist matters.
  • Some weeks are quiet.
  • Sellers sometimes don’t show up.
  • Not every phone is worth buying, so you will say no sometimes.

13. If it’s a fit, what happens next

  • Training 1 (about an hour): the job, the life of one phone, the app and the rules. Booked before you leave for uni.
  • Training 2: inspecting a phone properly, prices and deductions, with a phone in your hand.
  • First meetups start once the Sydney ads are ready, and when you’re settled in Sydney.
